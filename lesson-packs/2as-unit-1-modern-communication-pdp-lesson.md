# 2. Detailed 60-Minute Lesson Plan (PDP Structure)

> **Time budget — strict:** Pre 10 + While 30 + Post 15 + Exit 5 = **60 minutes**.

---

## PRE-TASK — 10 minutes

**Goal:** activate prior knowledge, generate curiosity. **No teaching, no vocabulary explanation.** Pure oral interaction.

### Step 1 — Visual stimulus (3 min)

Show two pictures side by side on the board (or print them):

- **Picture A:** an old paper letter in an envelope, OR a black-and-white landline telephone, OR a telegraph machine.
- **Picture B:** a smartphone showing a WhatsApp / Instagram screen, OR people on a video call.

Ask the class to **look only**. Give 30 seconds of silence.

### Step 2 — Prediction questions (7 min)

Run a quick whole-class oral exchange. Ask in this order:

1. "What can you see in Picture A? In Picture B?"
2. "How did people communicate 50 years ago?"
3. "How do you communicate with your friends today?"
4. "Which one is faster? Which one is more personal?"
5. "What do you think the lesson today is about?"

**Teacher behaviour:** collect ideas on the board in two columns (PAST | TODAY). Do **not** correct grammar. Do **not** pre-teach vocabulary. Just listen and acknowledge.

> **Rule of thumb:** if a student is talking, you are succeeding. End the pre-task when the board is full or at exactly 10 minutes — whichever comes first.

---

## WHILE-TASK — 30 minutes

**Goal:** comprehension of the reading text. **No teacher explanation inside the text work.** Students read and answer; the teacher monitors silently.

Hand out **Worksheet 1 (Reading Comprehension)** with the text on top.

### Task 1 — Global comprehension (5 min)

Students read the text **once** silently. Then answer **one** global question:

> *Tick the best title for the text:*
> ☐ a) The Internet today
> ☐ b) How communication has changed
> ☐ c) The history of telephones

Quick whole-class check (30 seconds). Move on.

### Task 2 — Detailed comprehension #1: True / False (8 min)

Students read again and complete **5 True/False statements** (on the worksheet). They must **correct the false ones** in writing.

Pair-check (2 min) → whole-class check (1 min).

### Task 3 — Detailed comprehension #2: Wh-questions (8 min)

Students answer **5 short Wh-questions** in writing, using complete sentences pulled from the text.

Pair-check → whole-class check.

### Task 4 — Text-based vocabulary (9 min)

Students complete a **6-item vocabulary task** on Worksheet 2 — **matching the word to its meaning using only context from the text**. They are not to use a dictionary at this stage.

Whole-class check; teacher writes the 6 target words on the board for visibility.

> **Reminder:** during all four tasks the teacher does **not** explain. The teacher only nudges (*"Look at line 3"* / *"Read the sentence again"*) and monitors who is on / off task.

---

## POST-TASK — 15 minutes

**Goal:** speaking — students express their own preference and justify it. Use the language they have just met.

### Step 1 — Set the question (1 min)

Write on the board:

> **"Which way of communicating do you prefer — old (letters, landline) or modern (mobile, Internet)? Why?"**

### Step 2 — Pair work with scaffold (8 min)

Hand out **Worksheet 3 (Speaking Scaffold)**. Students work in **pairs**:

- Each student speaks for 1–2 minutes using the sentence starters on the worksheet.
- The partner listens and asks **one follow-up question** from the worksheet.
- Roles swap.

The scaffold provides:
- Opinion starters (*"I prefer... because..."*)
- Justification phrases (*"It's faster / cheaper / more personal..."*)
- Agreement / disagreement (*"I agree because... / I don't agree because..."*)

### Step 3 — Open-class share (6 min)

Call on **4–5 students** to share their preference and one reason. Encourage one peer to react with *"I agree / I don't agree because..."*.

> **Differentiation tip:** if a student struggles, offer them only the opinion starter and ask them to give one reason. Praise all attempts.

---

## EXIT TICKET — 5 minutes

**Goal:** assessable individual written production.

Hand out **Worksheet 4 (Exit Ticket Strip)**. Each student writes **3–4 sentences** answering:

> *"How was communication different in the past? Which way do you prefer? Why?"*

Students must use:
- One past form (e.g. `used to ...` or simple past)
- One present-tense sentence (about today)
- One opinion sentence

Collect the strips at the door as students leave. Mark using the **Success Criteria** in §8.

# 3. Reading Text

## Then and Now: How We Stay in Touch

> Fifty years ago, communication was very different. People used to write long letters by hand. They waited many days for a reply. Telephones were big and stayed in one place at home. Some families didn't even have a phone. To send an urgent message, people sent a telegram.
>
> Today, communication is fast and easy. We have mobile phones, the Internet, and social media. We can send a message to a friend in another country in one second. We can see and hear each other on video calls. We can share photos with many people on Facebook or Instagram.
>
> Modern communication is amazing. But some people miss the old days. They say letters were more personal.

*(117 words — A2 level)*

## Pair Work — *Which way of communicating do you prefer?*

**Step 1 — Think alone (1 minute):** which do you prefer — old ways (letters, landline phone) or modern ways (mobile, Internet, social media)? Why?

**Step 2 — Speak with your partner (1–2 minutes each):**
- Use the **sentence starters** below.
- Then your partner speaks.

**Step 3 — Ask a follow-up question** to your partner.

---

### Useful Sentence Starters

**To express a preference**
- "I prefer **modern / old** ways of communicating because ..."
- "In my opinion, ... is better because ..."
- "I think ..."

**To justify**
- "It's **faster** / **cheaper** / **easier** / **more personal**."
- "You can ..."
- "You don't have to ..."

**To agree / disagree with your partner**
- "I agree with you because ..."
- "I don't agree because ..."
- "That's a good point, but ..."

### Follow-up Questions

Choose **one** to ask your partner after they speak:

- "How often do you use a mobile phone every day?"
- "Have you ever written a real letter? To whom?"
- "Would you like to receive a letter from a friend? Why / why not?"
- "Do you think we will still write letters in 30 years?"

> **Print one copy per class.** Tick boxes during the lesson; a student with **3 or 4 ticks** has met today's target.

## 8.2 Success Criteria (share with students at the start)

By the end of the lesson, **I can:**

- ☐ identify two ways people **used to** communicate in the past
- ☐ identify two ways we communicate **today**
- ☐ understand a short text about communication
- ☐ tell my partner which way I prefer and **give one reason**
- ☐ write 3–4 sentences comparing past and present communication

## 8.3 Exit Ticket Expectation

A passing exit ticket contains:

- **3 or 4 sentences** (not 1 or 2);
- **at least one past form** (used to / simple past);
- **at least one present-tense sentence** about today;
- **one clear opinion** with the word *prefer* / *think* / *believe*.

Sentences may have minor grammar errors as long as the meaning is clear.
