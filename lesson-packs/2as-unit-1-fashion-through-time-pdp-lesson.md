# 2. 60-Minute Lesson Plan

> Strict timing: **Pre 10 + While 30 + Post 15 + Exit 5 = 60 minutes**.

---

## 🔵 PRE-TASK — 10 minutes

**Goal:** activate prior knowledge about fashion change. Spark curiosity. **No teaching, no vocabulary front-loading.**

### Step 1 — Visual stimulus (3 min)

Display three pictures side by side on the board (or print and pin):

- **Picture A — 1970s:** a family photo with a woman in a long dress / haïk and a man in a suit.
- **Picture B — 1990s:** young people in jeans and colourful t-shirts.
- **Picture C — Today:** a teenager in trendy sportswear or modern street fashion.

Give 30 seconds of silent observation.

### Step 2 — Prediction questions (7 min)

Whole-class oral exchange. Ask in this order:

1. "What do you see in Picture A? B? C?"
2. "When do you think Picture A was taken? Picture B? Picture C?"
3. "Who in your family wore clothes like Picture A?"
4. "What kinds of clothes do you wear today?"
5. "What do you think we're going to read about?"

Collect ideas on the board in three columns: **PAST | 1990s | TODAY**.

> **Teacher behaviour:** listen, repeat, encourage. Do **not** correct grammar. Do **not** translate or pre-teach the reading vocabulary. End the pre-task at exactly 10 minutes.

---

## 🟡 WHILE-TASK — 30 minutes

**Goal:** comprehension of the text. **The teacher does not explain inside text work** — only nudges and monitors.

Hand out **Worksheet 1**.

### Task 1 — Global comprehension (5 min)

Students read silently **once**. Tick the best title. Quick whole-class check.

### Task 2 — True / False (8 min)

Students read again and complete **5 True / False statements**. They must **correct the false ones** in writing.

Pair-check (2 min) → whole-class check (1 min).

### Task 3 — Wh-Questions (8 min)

Students answer **5 short questions** in writing, using complete sentences from the text.

Pair-check → whole-class check.

### Task 4 — Vocabulary in context (9 min)

Students complete **Worksheet 2** — match 6 words to their meanings using only context from the text. **No dictionary** at this stage.

Whole-class check; teacher writes the 6 target words on the board.

---

## 🟢 POST-TASK — 15 minutes

**Goal:** speaking — students express their own preference and justify it.

### Step 1 — Set the question (1 min)

Write on the board:

> **"Which fashion style do you like best — past, 1990s, or today? Why?"**

### Step 2 — Pair speaking with scaffold (9 min)

Hand out **Worksheet 3**. In pairs:

- Student A speaks for 1–2 minutes using the sentence starters.
- Student B listens and asks one follow-up question.
- Roles swap.

### Step 3 — Open-class share (5 min)

Call on **4–5 students** to share their preference and one reason. Encourage one peer to react with *"I agree because..."* or *"I don't agree because..."*.

---

## ⚪ EXIT TICKET — 5 minutes

**Goal:** assessable individual written production.

Hand out **Exit Ticket Strips**. Each student writes **3–4 sentences** answering:

> *"How was fashion different in the past? Which style do you prefer? Why?"*

Required:
- one **past form** (`used to ...` or simple past)
- one **present-tense** sentence about today
- one **opinion** sentence (with *I prefer / I think / I like best*)

Collect at the door.

# 3. Reading Text

## Fifty Years of Fashion

> Fashion in Algeria has changed a lot in the last fifty years. In the 1970s, women used to wear long traditional dresses, and men used to wear suits or simple cotton shirts. Most clothes were made at home or by a local tailor.
>
> In the 1990s, jeans became very popular for young people. Boys wore t-shirts with English words on them. Girls liked colourful tops and tight jeans. Some teenagers tried to copy famous singers from TV.
>
> Today, fashion is very different. Many young people follow stars on Instagram and copy their style. Some wear sportswear every day. Others keep traditional dresses for weddings and special occasions.
>
> Fashion changes, but every style tells a story about its time.

*(118 words — A2 level)*

# 6. Worksheet 3 — Speaking Scaffold

> **Print one copy per student. Pair students before handing out.**

---

## Pair Work — *Which fashion style do you like best?*

**Step 1 — Think alone (1 min):** which style do you like best — **past (1970s), 1990s, or today**? Why?

**Step 2 — Speak with your partner (1–2 min each):** use the sentence starters below. Then your partner speaks.

**Step 3 — Ask a follow-up question** to your partner.

---

### Useful Sentence Starters

**To express a preference**
- "I like ... fashion best because ..."
- "In my opinion, ... clothes are nicer because ..."
- "I think ... style is more ..."

**To justify**
- "It's more **comfortable** / **modern** / **traditional** / **colourful**."
- "It looks ..."
- "It reminds me of ..."

**To agree / disagree with your partner**
- "I agree because ..."
- "I don't agree because ..."
- "That's true, but I prefer ..."

---

### Follow-up Questions *(choose ONE to ask your partner)*

- "Do you have a photo of your parents in old clothes? What do they wear?"
- "What do you wear for a wedding or a special occasion?"
- "Do you like to follow fashion stars on Instagram?"
- "Do you prefer modern or traditional clothes for everyday life?"

# 8. Teacher's Tools

## 8.1 Observation Checklist (use during the lesson)

| 4.       | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| 5.       | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| ...      | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

A student with **3 or 4 ticks** has met today's target.

## 8.2 Success Criteria *(share with students at the start)*

By the end of the lesson, **I can:**

- ☐ describe two ways people **used to** dress in the past
- ☐ describe how young people dress **today**
- ☐ understand a short text about fashion change
- ☐ tell my partner which style I prefer and **give one reason**
- ☐ write 3–4 sentences comparing past and present fashion

## 8.3 Exit Ticket Pass Criteria

A passing exit ticket contains:

- **3 or 4 sentences**
- at least one **past form** (`used to` or simple past)
- at least one **present-tense** sentence about today
- one clear **opinion** with *prefer / think / like best*

Minor grammar errors are acceptable as long as the meaning is clear.
