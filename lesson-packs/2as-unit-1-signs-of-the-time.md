# 1. Unit Overview

This unit invites learners to compare three slices of life: how people **used to live** in the past, how they **live today**, and how they **might live** in the future. By the end of the unit, students will produce a **Life Style Profile** — a short illustrated description of past / present / future lifestyles drawn from their own family and community.

The unit naturally combines three high-leverage grammar items: **`used to`** (past habits), **`going to`** (planned future), and **`may / might / will`** (degrees of certainty about the future). It builds reading, writing, listening and speaking together rather than separately.

---

# 2. Learning Outcomes (CBA-Aligned)

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

1. **Describe** people's past habits and customs using `used to` (affirmative, negative, interrogative).
2. **Compare and contrast** life in the past with life today using lifestyle vocabulary.
3. **Predict** future lifestyle changes using `going to`, `may`, `might` and `will`.
4. **Recognise and use** at least 12 lifestyle vocabulary items in context.
5. **Express opinions** about lifestyle change with simple agree / disagree phrases.
6. **Write** a short descriptive paragraph (60–80 words) comparing past and present.
7. **Present** their Life Style Profile orally to a partner or small group.

# 3. Language Focus

## Grammar

### Primary: `used to` for past habits

| Form | Example |
|------|---------|
| Affirmative | People **used to wear** traditional clothes. |
| Negative | They **didn't use to have** mobile phones. |
| Interrogative | **Did people use to** travel by camel? — *Yes, they did.* |

> **Quick rule:** `used to + base verb` describes a habit or state in the past that is no longer true today. Use the bare infinitive *use* (not *used*) after `did` and `didn't`.

### Secondary: `going to` and modals for future predictions

| Structure | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| `be going to + verb` | Planned / very likely future | People **are going to use** electric cars. |
| `will + verb` | Certain prediction | Robots **will help** in many homes. |
| `may / might + verb` | Less certain prediction | We **might travel** to other planets one day. |

## Vocabulary (15 words)

| Word | Type | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| fashion | noun | popular style of clothes or hair |
| tradition | noun | a custom passed from one generation to the next |
| custom | noun | a usual way of doing something in a society |
| habit | noun | something a person does regularly |
| modern | adjective | of the present or recent times |
| ancient | adjective | very old; from a long time ago |
| lifestyle | noun | the way a person or group lives |
| entertainment | noun | activities people do for fun |
| ago | adverb | in the past (e.g. *ten years ago*) |
| nowadays | adverb | in the present time |
| invent | verb | to create something that did not exist before |
| discover | verb | to find or learn about something for the first time |
| predict | verb | to say what will happen in the future |
| progress | noun | forward movement; improvement |
| change | noun / verb | becoming different |

## Functional Expressions

**Talking about the past**
- "People used to ..."
- "Long ago / In the old days, families ..."
- "My grandparents tell me that ..."

**Comparing past and present**
- "In the past ..., but today ..."
- "These days, people ... whereas before ..."
- "Things have changed because ..."

**Predicting the future**
- "I think / I believe ..."
- "It's likely that ..."
- "Maybe / Perhaps / Probably ..."
- "In ten years, we might ..."

**Giving opinions**
- "In my opinion, ..."
- "I (don't) agree because ..."
- "That's a good point, but ..."

# 4. Lesson Plans

> **Format used in every lesson:** Objective · Warm-up · Main Activity · Practice Task · Short Production Task. Total time per lesson: **60 minutes**.

---

## Lesson 1 — Life in the Past (`used to`)

**Objective:** Students will be able to describe past habits and customs using `used to` in affirmative and negative forms.

### Warm-up — *Then & Now Picture Match* (5 min)

Show two pictures side by side: a black-and-white photo of a 1950s village (no electricity, traditional dress) and a colour photo of a modern city (cars, smartphones). Ask:

- "Which picture shows the past? Which shows today?"
- "What do you see in each one?"

Collect 6–8 items on the board (e.g. *traditional clothes · oil lamp · horse · skyscraper · cars · phones*).

### Main Activity — *The "I used to..." Story* (20 min)

1. Tell a short personal story on the board:
   > *"When I was a child, I **used to walk** to school every day. I **didn't use to have** a computer. My family **used to listen** to the radio in the evening."*

2. Underline `used to` / `didn't use to`. Elicit the rule:
   - **Past habits that are no longer true today.**
   - Form: `used to + base verb`.
   - Negative: `didn't use to + base verb`.

3. Drill the form orally with chain examples around the class:
   *"I used to ___ when I was 10."* (sleep early · play marbles · watch cartoons · drink milk every morning · etc.)

### Practice Task — *Grandparents' Lives* (15 min)

Hand out (or write on the board) the following gap-fill paragraph:

> My grandmother is 75 years old. When she was young, she ___ (wear) traditional dresses every day. She ___ (not / have) a fridge or a TV. Families ___ (eat) together on the floor. Children ___ (play) outside in the streets. They ___ (not / use) computers because computers ___ (not / exist).

Students complete in pairs, then check answers as a class. (Answers in the **Answer Key** below.)

### Short Production Task — *My Family's Past* (15 min)

Students write **4–5 sentences** about their parents' or grandparents' youth, using `used to` at least three times.

Sentence frames on the board:
- *My mother / father / grandfather used to ...*
- *In their village / town, people used to ...*
- *They didn't use to ...*

Volunteers read aloud at the end.

### Homework

Bring **one photo** of a family member from the past (a grandparent, an old family picture). Be ready to describe it in Lesson 2.

---

## Lesson 2 — Life Today (Comparing Past & Present)

**Objective:** Students will be able to compare past and present lifestyles using lifestyle vocabulary and contrast expressions.

### Warm-up — *Photo Talk* (5 min)

Pair students. Each shows their family photo and says **one sentence** about what people used to do in that picture. Quick whip-around: each pair shares one observation.

### Main Activity — *Then vs. Now Chart* (20 min)

Draw a 2-column chart on the board:

| Past (used to) | Today |
|----------------|-------|
| | |

Brainstorm together under three headings:
- **Clothes** — *traditional dress / kandoura / jellaba* vs *jeans / t-shirts*
- **Food** — *home-made bread, couscous, fresh vegetables* vs *fast food, ready meals*
- **Entertainment** — *family stories, radio* vs *TV, internet, mobile games*

Introduce contrast expressions on the board:
- *"In the past ..., but today ..."*
- *"People used to ..., whereas now they ..."*
- *"These days ..."*

### Practice Task — *Sentence Builder* (15 min)

Students write **6 contrast sentences** in pairs, using items from the chart. One sentence per category, twice.

Example:
> *In the past, families used to eat home-made food, but today many people eat fast food.*

### Short Production Task — *Mini Paragraph: Life in My Town* (15 min)

Students write a **5–6 sentence paragraph** comparing life in their town 30 years ago and today. They must use:

- `used to` at least twice
- one of the contrast phrases (*in the past..., today...* / *whereas* / *these days*)
- two vocabulary items from the unit list

Collect for review (or use as homework).

---

## Lesson 3 — Life in the Future (`going to`, `may`, `might`, `will`)

**Objective:** Students will be able to make predictions about future lifestyles using `going to` and modals of certainty.

### Warm-up — *Crystal Ball* (5 min)

Ask:
- "What do you think life will be like in 30 years?"
- "What will be the same? What will be different?"

Collect 5–6 ideas on the board (flying cars · robots in homes · trips to space · paperless schools · etc.).

### Main Activity — *Sure, Maybe, Probably* (20 min)

Introduce the certainty scale on the board:

```
100% sure   ----- will / are going to -----   I am sure ...
70% sure    -------------- probably -------   It's likely that ...
50% sure    ---- may / might / could -----   Maybe / Perhaps ...
```

Give 6 example predictions. Ask students to place each on the scale and justify with the matching modal:

1. People ___ travel to Mars one day.
2. Cars ___ fly in the sky.
3. Phones ___ be smaller and faster.
4. Schools ___ use paper books.
5. Robots ___ cook in our kitchens.
6. People ___ live to be 150 years old.

Class discussion. Encourage justifications: *"I think probably, because scientists are already working on it."*

### Practice Task — *Predict the Future Worksheet* (15 min)

Students complete the following table individually:

| Topic | A prediction (with `going to` or modal) |
|-------|------------------------------------------|
| Clothes | |
| Food | |
| Transport | |
| Entertainment | |
| Schools | |

After 8 minutes, pair students to compare. Each pair picks the **two most interesting predictions** and reads them to the class.

### Short Production Task — *3-Sentence Future* (15 min)

Each student writes **3 predictions** about life in 2050:

- 1 prediction with `will` (you are sure)
- 1 prediction with `may / might` (you are not sure)
- 1 prediction with `going to` (you have evidence)

Share orally in groups of 4.

---

## Lesson 4 — Project Day & Assessment

**Objective:** Students will produce a **Life Style Profile** combining past, present and future descriptions.

### Warm-up — *Three-Word Recap* (5 min)

Each student writes **three words** on a slip of paper: one for *past lifestyle*, one for *present lifestyle*, one for *future lifestyle*. Quick share-out.

### Main Activity — *Life Style Profile Construction* (35 min)

Students work individually on their **Life Style Profile**. The profile contains three short paragraphs (50–70 words each):

1. **Past** — How people used to live in your family / town.
2. **Present** — How people live today (comparing with the past).
3. **Future** — How you imagine people will live in 30 years.

Each paragraph must include:
- At least **one target grammar item** (`used to` for paragraph 1; contrast phrase for paragraph 2; future form for paragraph 3)
- At least **two lifestyle vocabulary items** from the unit list
- A small picture or sketch (drawn or printed) representing the time period

While students write, the teacher circulates: nudge with sentence starters, correct on the spot, encourage vocabulary recycling.

### Production Task — *Mini-Presentation* (15 min)

In groups of 3–4, students share their Life Style Profile. Each presenter reads the three paragraphs aloud (about 2 minutes per student). Group members ask **one question** each.

### Submission

Students submit the written Life Style Profile to the teacher. Assessment is against the rubric in §5.

### Homework

None — the project IS the homework outcome.

# 5. Assessment Task & Rubric

## The Task

> **Life Style Profile** — Write three short paragraphs (50–70 words each) describing how people **used to live** in the past, how they **live today**, and how they **might live** in 30 years. Include at least one picture per period.

**Total length:** 150–200 words across three paragraphs.

## Rubric (4 levels · 4 criteria)

| Criterion | **Excellent** (4) | **Good** (3) | **Developing** (2) | **Beginning** (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content & ideas** | All three time periods are clearly described with rich, accurate detail. Pictures match the paragraphs. | Three periods are described; some details are missing or general. Pictures are relevant. | Two of the three periods are clearly described; the third is thin. Pictures present but loosely connected. | Only one period is developed. Pictures missing or unrelated. |
| **Use of target grammar** | Uses `used to`, contrast phrases, and future forms (`going to` / modals) accurately and naturally. | Uses all three structures with mostly correct form. Minor errors. | Uses some target structures but with frequent form errors. | Few or no target structures used; basic present-tense sentences. |
| **Vocabulary range** | Uses 6+ unit vocabulary items appropriately. Word choice is varied. | Uses 4–5 unit vocabulary items appropriately. | Uses 2–3 unit vocabulary items, sometimes inaccurately. | Fewer than 2 unit vocabulary items used. |
| **Organisation & accuracy** | Three clear paragraphs with linkers. Spelling and punctuation are accurate. Easy to read. | Three paragraphs, mostly clear. A few spelling / punctuation errors. | Paragraphs run together or unclear. Several errors that distract the reader. | No paragraph structure. Errors block meaning. |

**Total:** 16 points → convert to 20 by multiplying by 1.25, or use the band as a holistic mark.

# 6. Teacher's Tools

## 6.1 Delivery Tips

- **Anchor every lesson in the students' own lives.** Photos, family stories, and local examples make `used to` come alive far more than textbook gap-fills.
- **Drill the form, then move on.** `Used to` is structurally simple — five minutes of choral drilling at the start of Lesson 1 saves a lot of correction later.
- **Pair work is your friend.** All four lessons have a pair / group stage. Pair stronger students with weaker ones for the Practice Tasks; mix friend-groups for the Production Tasks.
- **Build a class word wall.** Stick the 15 vocabulary items on the wall in three columns (Past / Present / Future). Students add their own words as the unit progresses.
- **Use the board strategically.** Reserve one corner for the contrast expressions; one corner for the certainty scale. Don't erase them between lessons.

## 6.2 Common Student Difficulties

| Difficulty | What to do |
|---|---|
| Saying "*I used to went*" or "*Did you used to go?*" | After `did` / `didn't`, the form is **`use to`** (no -d). Drill: *"Did you USE to..." (clap)* |
| Confusing `used to` (past habit) with `to be used to` (be accustomed to) | Don't introduce the second meaning at this level. Stay with past-habit only. |
| Mixing up `going to` and `will` | Use the certainty scale: `going to` = there's evidence (a plan, a sign); `will` = pure prediction or decision-on-the-spot. |
| Vocabulary recall — students recognise words but don't reuse them | Insist on minimum vocabulary counts in the Production Tasks. The rubric rewards variety. |
| Short, present-tense answers in Production Tasks | Provide sentence frames on the board. Insist on full sentences in writing. |

## 6.3 Differentiation (Three Levels)

**Struggling students**
- Provide a sentence-frame handout for every Production Task (e.g. *"My grandfather used to ____. He didn't use to ____. Today, he ____."*)
- Allow them to use 8 vocabulary items instead of 12 in the assessment.
- Pair with a stronger partner for peer support during writing.

**On-level students**
- Follow the lesson as written.
- Encourage them to add a fourth paragraph in the project: *"What do I want to keep from the past?"*

**Advanced students**
- Set them a stretch task: write a short dialogue (10 lines) between a grandparent and a grandchild discussing how life has changed.
- Ask them to teach the certainty scale to a small group.
- Encourage use of less-common modals: *could*, *should*.

# 7. Answer Key

## Lesson 1 — *Grandparents' Lives* gap-fill

> My grandmother is 75 years old. When she was young, she **used to wear** traditional dresses every day. She **didn't use to have** a fridge or a TV. Families **used to eat** together on the floor. Children **used to play** outside in the streets. They **didn't use to use** computers because computers **didn't use to exist**.

## Lesson 3 — *Sure, Maybe, Probably* sample answers

(Many answers possible — these are typical.)

1. People **may / might** travel to Mars one day. *(50% sure — not yet possible)*
2. Cars **may / might** fly in the sky. *(50% — uncertain)*
3. Phones **will / are going to** be smaller and faster. *(very sure — clear trend)*
4. Schools **may / might** use paper books. *(uncertain — some still do)*
5. Robots **will / are going to** cook in our kitchens. *(likely — already starting)*
6. People **may / might** live to be 150 years old. *(uncertain — debated)*

Accept any modal that the student justifies sensibly with reference to the certainty scale.

## Lesson 4 — Sample Life Style Profile (model answer)

> **Past.** My grandparents used to live in a small village. They didn't use to have electricity, so they used oil lamps at night. The whole family used to eat couscous together every Friday. Women used to wear traditional dresses and men used to work in the fields from dawn to sunset.
>
> **Present.** Today, life has changed a lot. We live in a big city and we have electricity, internet and modern kitchens. People still eat couscous on Fridays, but we also eat pizza and fast food. Children go to school by bus, whereas before they used to walk for an hour.
>
> **Future.** In 30 years, I think life is going to be very different. We will probably have flying cars and smart homes. Robots may help in the kitchen. Schools might not use paper books any more — students will read on tablets. But I hope the tradition of Friday couscous will stay.

(189 words. Uses `used to` ×6, contrast phrases ×2, `will` ×2, `going to` ×1, `may / might` ×2, and 9 unit vocabulary items.)
